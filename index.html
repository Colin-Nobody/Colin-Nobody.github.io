About

AI methods for measuring visual pollution

I am pursuing a PhD in Electrical and Electronic Engineering (EEE) at the University of Nottingham Ningbo China. In my GUVP project, I investigate how AI can identify visual elements in street-view imagery, relate them to human perception, and support scalable assessment of urban visual pollution.

GUVP · Current research

An AI pipeline for street-scene assessment

The project brings together complementary model families: VLMs provide pairwise perceptual judgements, segmentation models expose scene composition, and regression methods explore how semantic features relate to visual-pollution scores. A more efficient ViT-based evaluator is a future research direction.

Vision-language models

Evaluation pipeline

Use Qwen vision-language models to compare street-view images for perceived cleanliness and visual pollution. Evidence-based scoring and balanced A/B–B/A evaluation help audit and reduce image-position bias.

Pairwise preference · MEC · BPC

Semantic segmentation

Baseline trained

Train and evaluate a DeepLabV3+ model on Mapillary Vistas v2 street scenes. Pixel-level semantic predictions provide interpretable class proportions and urban-scene features for downstream analysis.

DeepLabV3+ · Mapillary Vistas v2

Regression modelling

Feature-to-score analysis

Explore regression models that relate segmentation-derived semantic features to visual-pollution score targets, investigating whether scene composition can help explain and predict perceived pollution.

Semantic features · Continuous score prediction

Vision Transformer (ViT)

Future direction

Investigate a more efficient ViT-based pairwise evaluator as a potential alternative to repeated multi-pass VLM inference. The goal is to retain useful preference signals while reducing inference cost; this is planned work, not a completed replacement.
